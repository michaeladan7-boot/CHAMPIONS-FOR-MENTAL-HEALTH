Jesse Ofori

Co-Founder | Mental Health Advocate | Lived Experience Researcher

Jesse Ofori is a Ghanaian public service professional, mental health advocate, and lived experience researcher with over a decade of involvement in mental health support groups, advocacy, and advisory work. He is passionate about promoting mental health awareness, challenging stigma and discrimination, and advancing the meaningful participation of persons with lived experience in research, policy, and community development. One of his key areas of interest is addressing tokenism in mental health advocacy and research, particularly the need to move beyond symbolic representation towards genuine participation, influence, and shared decision-making.

Jesse is a Co-Founder of Champions for Mental Health International, where he is committed to advancing mental health advocacy, empowerment, peer support and research, and inclusion for persons living with mental health conditions. He is a member of the Lived Experience Advisory Board (LEAB) of The Lancet Psychiatry Commission on Lived Experience in Mental Health Research, contributing lived experience perspectives to global discussions on mental health research, co-production, and meaningful involvement. He also serves as a peer reviewer of research papers for The Lancet Psychiatry.

He is a Research Associate with Traumascapes, London, and is affiliated with King’s College London through his involvement in collaborative mental health research. His research work includes managing research projects, coordinating activities, timelines, stakeholder engagement, and deliverables, while advocating for research approaches that meaningfully integrate lived experience.

His commitment to mental health extends to community involvement as an Executive Member of PsychoSocial Africa, a grassroots advocacy and support group in Ghana, and through his involvement with the Accra Participatory Group, a peer research network in Accra.

Jesse is dedicated to amplifying the voices of persons with lived experience, strengthening peer-led initiatives, and contributing to a mental health landscape where lived experience is recognised as expertise and people are empowered to live fulfilling, meaningful, and productive lives. He is particularly interested in challenging tokenistic approaches to lived experience engagement and promoting inclusive practices that recognise people with lived experience as equal partners in shaping the research, policies, and services that affect them. He is Ghanaian and resides in Accra, Ghana.
